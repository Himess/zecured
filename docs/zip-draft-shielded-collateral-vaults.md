# Terminology

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "SHOULD" and "MAY" are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14
when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.

- **Vault**: a shielded address controlled by a threshold of signers, holding ZEC that backs a
  loan on another chain.
- **Signer set**: the *n* signers of a vault and the threshold *t* of them that must cooperate to
  spend from it. This draft uses *n* = 5 and *t* = 3.
- **Settlement chain**: the chain where the loan lives and where the signers' commitments and
  any proofs of misbehaviour are checked. The reference implementation uses Base, an EVM chain.
- **Owner**: the borrower, identified on the settlement chain by a fresh key per loan.
- **Denomination**: the exact value a vault accepts as its deposit.
- **Conforming deposit**: the one note per vault that backs the loan (§ Deposit classification).
- **Lineage**: the conforming deposit and every change note a vault's payouts return to it. At
  any time it is a single unspent note.
- **Claim**: a right, created by an event on the settlement chain, to be paid an amount from a
  vault: a release to the owner, a liquidation, or the two parts of a close.
- **Recipient commitment**: `keccak256(salt ‖ receiver)`, a claim's recipient as the settlement
  chain sees it.
- **Tag key**, **deposit tag**, **payout reference** and **claim reference**: the keyed or salted
  values the settlement chain uses in place of Zcash data (§ Tags and references).

# Abstract

This ZIP describes a protocol for keeping ZEC in the Ironwood shielded pool while it secures a
loan on another chain. Each loan gets its own threshold vault. Its keys are generated by a
distributed key generation, so no signer ever holds a vault's spending key. The vault accepts
exactly one deposit, under deterministic rules that every signer applies alike. A deposit can be
proven to the settlement chain with a zero-knowledge proof that reveals no Zcash transaction,
note or address. The signers pay out only against claims created on the settlement chain, and
they commit every payout there before releasing their signature shares, so that a payout the
commitment does not describe can be proven and penalized. No Zcash transaction id, note
commitment or address ever appears on the settlement chain.

# Motivation

Shielded ZEC is the asset Zcash users most want to keep private, and the one they can least use
elsewhere. Every bridge or swap that takes ZEC to another chain today goes through a transparent
address or a pool crossing, which reveals amounts and links the user's history. Lending does not
need ZEC to move: a loan needs collateral that can be seized if the loan goes bad, and that can
be returned when it is repaid.

A threshold vault inside the shielded pool can play that role, provided that:
- the chain that holds the loan can trust that the collateral exists;
- it can trust that the vault releases collateral only as the loan's state allows;
- nothing about the vault leaks onto the other chain.

This ZIP writes down the rules a vault and its signers follow. That lets independent
implementations interoperate. Wallets can verify vaults they are asked to pay. Watchers can
check, and prove, that signers follow the rules. The protocol is application-level: it needs no
consensus change.

# Privacy Implications

- **The deposit, every payout and every refund are fully shielded Ironwood transactions.** The
  protocol MUST NOT accept a deposit with a transparent input, a Sapling spend or an
  Orchard-pool action. It MUST NOT pay to any other pool. Pool crossings reveal amounts through
  the turnstile.
- **The settlement chain learns nothing that links to Zcash.** It learns:
  - a vault identifier, the loan's account and the denomination;
  - keyed tags of the vault's notes (§ Tags and references), which link to nothing without the
    vault's viewing key;
  - salted commitments of claim recipients, which link to nothing without their salts;
  - for a deposit proven in zero knowledge, a later block header (the anchor), never the
    deposit's block.
- **What remains linkable.** The loan itself is public on the settlement chain: its size,
  interest and any liquidation. So is the timing between a commitment and its payout on Zcash
  (see Commit before share; a random delay narrows it). A small anonymity set (Testnet) weakens
  every timing argument.
- **The signers see the vault.** They see its notes and the destinations it pays. This is
  inherent: they verify every payout against those notes. Third-party outputs (liquidators,
  market makers) MUST use no outgoing viewing key, so the owner's view of the vault does not
  reveal them.
- **Vault notes are quantum-recoverable.** Vault keys follow ZIP 2005 with `use_qsk = true`
  (§ Vault keys).

# Requirements

- A vault's ZEC never leaves the Ironwood pool.
- No single signer, and no set of fewer than *t* signers, can move a vault's ZEC.
- Every signer can decide independently, from public chain data and the vault's viewing key,
  which deposit backs a vault and what each payout must look like.
- The settlement chain can check that a vault was funded without trusting the signers or a light
  wallet server, and without learning which Zcash transaction funded it.
- A payout that takes value backing the loan, other than as the settlement chain's claims allow,
  is provable on the settlement chain.

# Specification

## Vault keys

A vault's keys MUST come from a distributed key generation among its *n* signers, using
FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) as in ZIP 312, with re-randomized signing for spend authorization. The
signers MUST agree on the ZIP 2005 spending-key material `sk` by commit-reveal and derive the
vault's viewing keys with `use_qsk = true`, following ZIP 2005's guidance for FROST. A vault's
published address MUST be a unified address whose only receiver is the Orchard-protocol receiver
at diversifier index 0 of the external scope. Change returns to the internal scope.

When a signer completes the key generation, it MUST sign, with its key on the settlement chain:
- the vault's identifier, owner, account and denomination;
- the deposit expiry height;
- the hash of the vault's unified address and the hash of its unified full viewing key.

The owner MUST accept a vault only if every signer of a set it already trusts signed it.

## Deposit classification

Every signer MUST classify the notes received at the vault's external address in chain order:
by block height, then transaction index, then action index. The first note that meets all of
these rules is the conforming deposit, and it is the only note that can back the loan:
- the transaction that created it has only an Ironwood bundle, with no transparent, Sapling or
  Orchard-pool part;
- the note pays exactly the vault's published receiver;
- its value equals the denomination;
- it was mined at or below the deposit expiry height;
- it has at least *N* confirmations. *N* SHOULD be 10, ZIP 315's depth for an untrusted TXO.

Classification stops at the first note that has fewer than *N* confirmations, so a decision
never changes short of a reorganization deeper than *N*. Every other externally received note
is non-conforming. It belongs to its depositor and can leave the vault only through a refund that
the owner requests.

## Tags and references

With `UFVK` the vault's unified full viewing key string and `cmx` a note commitment:

| Value | Definition |
|---|---|
| tag key `K` | `BLAKE2b-256(personal = "Zecured_TagKey__", UFVK)` |
| deposit tag, lineage tag | `BLAKE2b-256(key = K, personal = "Zecured_DepTag__", cmx)` |
| payout reference | `BLAKE2b-256(key = K, personal = "Zecured_PayRef__", txid)` |
| recipient commitment | `keccak256(salt ‖ receiver)`, `salt` 32 random bytes, `receiver` the raw 43-byte Orchard-protocol receiver |
| claim reference | `keccak256(salt ‖ txid)`, with the salt of the claim's recipient commitment |

A txid is the ZIP 244 digest as modified for v6 transactions by ZIP 229, in internal byte order.

## Activation

The settlement chain activates a vault on its conforming deposit in one of two ways:
- **By attestation:** at least *t* signers sign the vault identifier, account, amount and deposit
  tag.
- **By proof:** a zero-knowledge proof shows all of the following:
  - a transaction that meets the classification rules creates a note of the denomination to the
    vault's published receiver;
  - that transaction is in a block whose ZIP 221 history-tree leaf is committed by a later
    header (the anchor);
  - the anchor is on the best chain of a header relay that checks Equihash and the difficulty
    rules, under a minimum amount of work.

  The proof's public output holds the anchor, the vault identifier, the account, the amount, the
  deposit tag and the hash of the message every signer signed at the vault's creation. Activation
  SHOULD wait some blocks and time after the proof, and MUST NOT proceed if the anchor leaves the
  best chain meanwhile.

A deposit tag and a vault identifier MUST each be used at most once.

## Claims and payouts

Claims are created only by settlement-chain events: a release after repayment, a partial release
within the loan's limits, a liquidation, or a close in which a third party repays the debt in
exchange for part of the collateral. Every signer MUST derive the payout plan independently from
those events and its own view of the vault, and MUST verify the proposed transaction against it.
The rules:
- **Shape:** one input, the current lineage note. The outputs are each claim's payment, to the
  receiver of its recipient commitment, plus the change back to the vault's internal address.
- **Fee:** the ZIP 317 fee, deducted from the first claim's payment.
- **Outgoing viewing keys:** the owner's payments use the vault's external outgoing viewing key;
  third-party payments use none; the change uses the internal one.
- **Receivers:** no two outputs of a payout pay the same receiver.
- **Expiry:** the transaction SHOULD expire 120 blocks after the height it is built for
  (ZIP 203's proposed default for NU7).
- **One payout at a time:** a signer MUST NOT sign a second payout while one it signed can still
  be mined.

A claim is paid once. A payout that expires unmined is rebuilt for the same claims.

## Commit before share

In a fully shielded v6 transaction the spend authorization digest is the txid, so every signer
knows the payout's txid in the first FROST round. Before releasing any second-round share, the
signers MUST record the payout on the settlement chain. Each signer signs the commitment with its
settlement-chain key:

    SpendCommit(vaultId, inputTag, claimIds[], claimRefs[], claimOutputs[], payoutRef, changeTag, changeValue)

- `inputTag` is the lineage tag of the note spent.
- `claimOutputs[i]` is the index of the action whose output pays claim *i*. The indexes are
  distinct.
- `changeTag` and `changeValue` describe the change note.

The settlement chain MUST check:
- at least *t* distinct signer signatures;
- that the input is a lineage note of the vault;
- that every claim is pending, belongs to the vault and is bound to no other input;
- conservation: the input's value equals the claims' amounts plus the change value;
- the ZIP 317 fee implied by the committed shape.

A signer MUST release its share only after seeing the record, with the signatures it signed, at
some depth on more than one independent provider.

Three statements, each provable in zero knowledge against an anchor header, then show a violation
without revealing any Zcash data:
- **Uncommitted spend:** a transaction reveals the nullifier of a lineage note, and no commitment
  names that note as the transaction's input. Anyone with the vault's viewing key can prove it.
- **Withheld change:** a committed transaction has no output that carries the committed change
  tag and decrypts, under the vault's incoming viewing keys, to the committed change value.
  Anyone with the viewing key can prove it.
- **Misdirected payment:** the committed output for a claim pays the claim's exact receiver less
  than the claim's expected amount. The claimant proves it with its own incoming viewing key; the
  note counts only if it matches the action's note commitment.

A proven violation SHOULD forfeit the signers' bonds:
- for an uncommitted spend, all signers, since a threshold signature does not show which signers
  took part;
- for the others, the commitment's signers.

## Refunds

A non-conforming note MAY be refunded to an Ironwood receiver that the owner names in a signed
request. The conforming deposit of a vault that was never activated MAY be refunded once the
settlement chain has recorded the vault as cancelled, which it MUST then never activate.

# Rationale

- **Ironwood only, exact denomination, one conforming note.** These rules make every signer's
  decision identical and keep amounts from revealing anything beyond the denomination. They also
  make the deposit statement small enough to prove.
- **Keyed and salted references.** The settlement chain needs stable identifiers for notes,
  payouts and recipients. Keying them with the vault's viewing key, or salting them with the
  recipient's secret, makes them useless to everyone else while letting the right party recompute
  and check them.
- **Commit before share.** A threshold signature does not show who signed it. A commitment made
  before the signature can exist turns "three signers could steal" into "a theft is provable",
  without any change to Zcash.
- **The ZIP 312 randomizer.** ZIP 312 derives the randomizer from the round-one commitments. A
  Zcash spend authorization signs `rk = ak + [alpha]G`, and the sighash commits to `rk`, so
  `alpha` must be fixed before round one. Implementations SHOULD draw `alpha` uniformly at random,
  have every signer recompute the sighash and `rk`, and send the randomizer only over a
  confidential channel.

# Deployment

This ZIP needs no consensus change. It applies to the Ironwood pool, so from NU6.3 on, with v6
transactions and the block-time and fee rules of the network the vault runs on.

# Reference implementation

Zecured (ZECATHON 2026), in Rust and Solidity:
- `signer/` holds the signer nodes and the coordinator;
- `contracts/` holds the settlement contracts;
- `tier2/` holds the deposit and violation statements and their RISC Zero guests.

# Open issues

- Liveness: signers can refuse to pay. A penalty for unpaid claims is not specified.
- Refunds of non-conforming notes are outside the violation statements.
- The fee formula is mempool policy. A change in policy needs the settlement chain's fee
  constants to change too, behind a delay.
- Confirmation depths and work thresholds must be set by the value at risk. On Testnet, proof of
  work gives no economic security.
- Independent signer operators, and replicated coordinators that remove the single point of
  censorship.
