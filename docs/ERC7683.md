# Close from collateral as a cross-chain intent (ERC-7683 mapping)

A close from collateral is a cross-chain intent. The borrower states an outcome: the debt on
Base is repaid, and the remaining ZEC comes back to a shielded address. A third party (our market
maker) fills it: it repays on Base and is paid in ZEC on Zcash. This page shows how that intent
maps onto ERC-7683's resolver model, which was rewritten in May 2026.

**Status: aligned with the ERC-7683 resolver draft (May 2026), not compliant.** There is no
resolver contract, no solver network integration and no open auction. The ZEC payment cannot be
expressed in the standard's vocabulary at all (see below).

## The order today

The borrower's smart account signs a `CloseRequest` (EIP-712, Adapter domain):

    CloseRequest(bytes32 vaultId, address account, address executor, uint16 marginBps,
                 uint64 minUserAmount, bytes32 userCommitment, uint256 nonce, uint64 deadline)

The executor then calls `SettlementAdapter.closeWithCollateral(req, sig, mmCommitment,
maxRepayAssets, minMmAmount)` on Base. This call:
1. repays the account's whole debt with USDC pulled from the executor;
2. withdraws and burns the collateral receipt;
3. records two claims in the Registry: the executor's (`mmAmount`, to `mmCommitment`) and the
   borrower's (the rest, to `userCommitment`).

The signer network then pays both claims in one shielded Ironwood transaction.

## Mapping onto the resolver model

ERC-7683 now describes an order by what `IResolver.resolve(bytes)` returns: a `ResolvedOrder`
with `steps`, `variables`, `payments` and `assumptions`. Addresses are ERC-7930 interoperable
addresses.

| `ResolvedOrder` part | A close from collateral |
|---|---|
| **Step** | `closeWithCollateral` on the `SettlementAdapter`, at `eip155:84532` (ERC-7930); attributes `SpendsERC20` (the USDC repaid), `TimingBounds(deadline)`, `RevertPolicy("abort")` |
| **Variables** | `StepCaller` (must equal `req.executor`); a `Query` for the account's Morpho debt (`maxRepayAssets`); a `Witness` for the filler's recipient commitment, which the filler gets by registering its Ironwood receiver with the signers (`POST /recipients`), so no Zcash address ever reaches Base; `QueryEvents(CloseRelease)` for the executed `mmAmount` |
| **Payments** | none expressible. The filler is paid in shielded ZEC on Zcash, and the resolver vocabulary has one payment type, `ERC20`, and no Zcash chain identifier. A non-standard extension would be needed |
| **Assumptions** | the filler is paid by 3 of the 5 signers, which commit the payout on Base before signing it (docs/TIER3.md); the payout queue is live; the price behind `marginBps` comes from the demo oracle |

## What a real solver network would need

- **Zcash identifiers.** CASA has no `zcash` namespace: the 49 namespaces include `bip122`,
  `eip155`, `solana` and `monero`, and the `bip122` profile does not cover unified addresses.
  Zcash needs a CAIP-2 chain id, CAIP-10 account strings for unified addresses, and a CAIP-350
  binary form for the raw 43-byte Orchard-protocol receiver.
- **A ZEC payment type**, settled by a shielded transaction and checked through the signers'
  commitment.
- **Open orders.** Today `executor` and `marginBps` are bound in the borrower's signature,
  because the order follows a quote. An open order would let any filler execute and compete on
  the margin.
- **Fillers with Ironwood wallets.**
